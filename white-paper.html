Introduction & Background

Across cultures and civilizations, humanity has sought answers to the same fundamental questions: Who am I? Why am I here? How do I heal? What is my purpose?

Modern systems have fragmented these inquiries into separate disciplines — medicine, psychology, education, spirituality, and art. This division has resulted in disconnection and confusion.

The Living Cosmology of Remembrance reunifies these domains into a cohesive framework. It recognizes human evolution as a spiral of becoming — one that integrates healing, identity, creation, and leadership into a unified journey.

This cosmology is embodied through books, teachings, and technologies designed to guide individuals from personal awakening to global impact.

Problem Statement

Fragmentation of Human Development

Contemporary systems treat individuals as collections of problems rather than integrated beings. The separation of mental, physical, emotional, and spiritual health into siloed professions means most people receive partial care — support for the symptom, not the whole person.

Limitations of Traditional Healthcare

Modern healthcare has made extraordinary advances in acute and emergency medicine, yet consistently underserves the chronic, emotional, and spiritual dimensions of wellbeing. Growing numbers of individuals report that conventional medicine addresses what is wrong without addressing who they are becoming.

Crisis of Identity

Many individuals experience confusion, disconnection, and lack of purpose in an increasingly complex world. The pace of cultural change has outstripped the frameworks most people carry for understanding themselves.

Outdated Educational Systems

Current educational models largely prioritize conformity, standardized assessment, and information delivery over identity development, creative leadership, and transformative self-knowledge.

Lack of Integrated Pathways

Few frameworks offer a comprehensive journey from healing to self-realization and legacy. Individuals must assemble their own path from fragmented sources — a process that often takes decades and lacks coherent direction.

Research Foundations & Evidence Base

The Living Cosmology of Remembrance is grounded in converging bodies of research across neuroscience, somatic psychology, transformative education, and market intelligence. The following section presents the substantive evidence supporting each domain of this work.

1. Neuroscience & Neuroplasticity

The brain's capacity for structural and functional change across the lifespan forms the scientific bedrock of transformation-based approaches.

The emerging field of neuroplasticity research — published across leading journals including Frontiers in Psychology, the Journal of Neuropsychiatry, and PubMed — consistently demonstrates that the brain continues to reorganize itself well beyond childhood, forming new neural connections in response to intentional practice, enriched environments, emotional learning, and therapeutic intervention.

  • A 2025 review published in Brain Sciences (MDPI) synthesizing research from 2015 to 2025 confirmed that neuroplasticity is a fundamental and lifelong property of the nervous system, enabling structural adaptation in response to learning, injury, and therapeutic engagement.

  • Research published in Frontiers in Psychology (Merzenich et al., 2016) documented that mindfulness, music, new learning environments, and physical activity all drive measurable neuroplastic gains — including increased gray matter volume and enhanced executive function — in adult populations.

  • An NIH/StatPearls review (Puderbaugh & Emmady, 2023) confirmed that music therapy, exercise, and cognitive enrichment are all associated with positive neuroplastic change, including delayed cognitive impairment averaging 8.7 years in highly engaged adult populations.

These findings directly support the framework offered by A Living Cosmology of Remembrance: identity-based, experiential, and emotionally engaged learning does not merely change what people know. It changes how the brain itself is organized.

Sources: Puderbaugh & Emmady, StatPearls, NIH, 2023; Merzenich et al., Frontiers in Psychology, 2016; MDPI Brain Sciences Review, 2025

2. Somatic Psychology & Body-Based Healing

A significant body of research now confirms the role of the body in processing trauma, regulating the nervous system, and facilitating emotional healing — providing evidence for somatic practices at the core of this cosmology.

  • A 2017 randomized controlled trial published in the Journal of Traumatic Stress (Brom et al.) — the first known RCT for Somatic Experiencing — found significant reductions in PTSD symptoms across 63 participants, positioning body-focused therapy as a distinct and effective complement to cognitive approaches.

  • A 2021 scoping literature review of 16 published studies on Somatic Experiencing (Kuhfuss et al., European Journal of Psychotraumatology) found preliminary evidence for positive effects on PTSD symptoms, with practitioners and clients identifying resource-orientation as a key healing factor.

  • A 2023 systematic meta-analysis of 29 body- and movement-oriented intervention studies for PTSD (van de Kamp et al., Journal of Traumatic Stress) demonstrated broad support for somatic approaches across diverse trauma presentations.

  • A 2024 scoping review of 43 studies (Leech et al., Frontiers in Psychiatry) found that interoceptive awareness — the practice of noticing and working with bodily sensations — is most consistently linked to emotion regulation, the very foundation of somatic integration practices.

The somatic practices embedded throughout the Living Cosmology of Remembrance — breathwork, body scanning, movement, and energetic integration — are not peripheral additions. They are evidence-informed tools for nervous system regulation and lasting transformation.

Sources: Brom et al., Journal of Traumatic Stress, 2017; Kuhfuss et al., European Journal of Psychotraumatology, 2021; van de Kamp et al., Journal of Traumatic Stress, 2023; Leech et al., Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2024

3. Identity-Based & Transformative Education

Jack Mezirow first introduced Transformative Learning Theory in 1978, proposing that the deepest form of adult learning is not informational but transformational — involving fundamental shifts in how a person understands themselves and their place in the world. In the decades since, this framework has become a cornerstone of adult education research.

  • Mezirow's theory has now accumulated nearly five decades of scholarly research, with meta-analyses confirming that transformative learning produces irreversible shifts in worldview, identity, behavior, epistemology, and capacity (Hoggan, 2016; New Directions for Adult and Continuing Education, 2024).

  • Research published in the Journal of Transformative Education (Rowan & Duerden, 2024) demonstrates that curricula designed around Transformative Learning Objectives — articulating specific shifts in ways of thinking, feeling, and being — significantly increase adult learners' long-term capacity to continue learning from lived experience.

  • A 2023 study in Punishment & Society (Lerman & Sadin) found measurable between- and within-subjects shifts in self-efficacy and civic orientation as a direct result of identity-centered educational engagement, demonstrating that identity framing shapes both learning outcomes and behavioral change.

The seven-gateway structure of A Living Cosmology of Remembrance is not arbitrary. It mirrors the stages of transformative learning identified in peer-reviewed educational theory: disorientation, critical reflection, identity shift, and integration. It offers what the research identifies as the conditions most likely to produce deep, lasting, identity-level change.

Sources: Mezirow, 1978; Hoggan, 2016; Rowan & Duerden, Journal of Transformative Education, 2024; Lerman & Sadin, Punishment & Society, 2023; New Directions for Adult and Continuing Education, 2024

4. Market Intelligence: The Wellness Economy

The commercial context for this work is one of extraordinary and sustained growth.

  • According to the Global Wellness Institute's 2025 Global Wellness Economy Monitor — the most authoritative annual research report on the sector — the global wellness economy reached $6.8 trillion in 2024, having doubled in size since 2013.

  • The wellness economy is now projected to approach $9.8 trillion by 2029, expanding at 7.6% annually — nearly double the projected global GDP growth rate of 4.5% (IMF forecasts).

  • Mental wellness is currently the second-fastest growing sector in the global wellness economy, expanding at 12.4% annually from 2019 to 2024 — tracking toward doubling in size by 2028.

  • Traditional and complementary medicine — the sector most closely aligned with frequency-based and energetic healing modalities — is forecast to grow at 10.8% annually through 2029.

  • The wellness economy is now larger than the global IT sector ($5.3 trillion), sports ($2.7 trillion), green economy ($5.1 trillion), and tourism ($5 trillion), and is almost four times larger than the global pharmaceutical industry.

These figures are not projections based on speculation. They represent measured, verified market data from the most comprehensive annual industry monitor in the wellness sector. The demand for integrated healing, personal transformation, and meaning-based wellbeing is not a trend. It is a structural shift in the global economy.

Source: Global Wellness Institute, Global Wellness Economy Monitor 2025, November 2025
